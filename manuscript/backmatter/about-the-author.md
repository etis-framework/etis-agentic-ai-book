# About the Author

**William T. O’Connell, Ph.D.**, is a computer scientist, former IBM Distinguished Engineer and Chief Technology Officer, and university educator. His four-decade career spans database research, distributed systems, enterprise architecture, and engineering leadership. His work asks a practical question: what must be true before an organization can depend on a complex system—not merely demonstrate it?

At **IBM**, O’Connell held two Chief Technology Officer (CTO) appointments, leading technology strategy for data warehousing, analytics, and big data, and later serving as CTO for Quality and Engineering Delivery Excellence in IBM Consulting, with technical responsibilities in an approximately **$15 billion** organization. Previously, at **AT&T Bell Laboratories and NCR/Teradata**, he worked on database engines, parallel and distributed systems, and high-performance data processing.

His contributions include **11 U.S. patents** and peer-reviewed research presented at **ACM SIGMOD, VLDB, and IEEE ICDE**. His technical work spans database query processing, parallel systems, information integration, architecture governance, and the engineering practices used to deliver and support complex enterprise platforms. His perspective combines hands-on technology development with responsibility for technical quality, architecture decisions, and the consequences of systems in operation. He earned his **Ph.D. in Computer Science from Illinois Institute of Technology**.

O’Connell teaches software engineering and advanced data structures and algorithms at **Loyola University Chicago**. Earlier appointments at **Princeton University** and the **University of Toronto** included advanced database courses. He developed the **ETIS Engineering Studio**, a repository-centered educational platform supporting evidence-based engineering review and accountable human judgment. His teaching emphasizes the ability to explain a design, execute tests, inspect evidence, identify uncertainty, and defend engineering decisions rather than merely produce working code.

He founded **Engineering Trustworthy Intelligent Systems (ETIS)** and authored its reference publications and **ETIS Professional Computing Series**. *Engineering Agentic AI Systems* draws on his research, technical leadership, and teaching to make a central point: **plausible agent decisions are not permission to act; trustworthy systems require independent control, verified evidence, recoverable state, and accountable people.**
