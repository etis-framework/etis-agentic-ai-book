# About the Author

**William T. O’Connell, Ph.D.**, is a computer scientist, former IBM Distinguished Engineer and Chief Technology Officer, and university educator whose work spans four decades of database research, distributed systems, enterprise architecture, and engineering leadership. His perspective is grounded in a practical question: what must be true before an organization can depend on a complex system—not merely demonstrate that it works?

At **IBM**, O’Connell held two Chief Technology Officer (CTO) roles. He led technology strategy for data warehousing, business intelligence, analytics, and big data, and later served as CTO for Quality and Engineering Delivery Excellence in IBM Consulting, with technical responsibilities in an approximately **$15 billion** business organization. His appointment as an **IBM Distinguished Engineer** recognized senior technical leadership in database architecture and enterprise systems. Earlier, at **AT&T Bell Laboratories and NCR/Teradata**, he worked on database engines and parallel and distributed data systems, including research in multimodal querying, massively parallel architectures, and high-performance database processing.

His technical contributions include **11 U.S. patents**, peer-reviewed publications in venues including **ACM SIGMOD, VLDB, and IEEE ICDE**, and work on database optimization, data-intensive platforms, and distributed programming. He earned his **Ph.D. in Computer Science from Illinois Institute of Technology**.

O’Connell teaches computer science at **Loyola University Chicago**, including software engineering and advanced data structures and algorithms. His earlier teaching appointments include **Princeton University** and the **University of Toronto**, with undergraduate and graduate instruction in database systems. His current work combines rigorous foundations with AI-assisted engineering, reproducible evidence, and independently evaluated engineering judgment. He also developed the **ETIS Engineering Studio**, a governed, repository-centered instructional review platform that supports engineering feedback while preserving accountable human decisions.

He founded **Engineering Trustworthy Intelligent Systems (ETIS)** and authored its reference publications and **ETIS Professional Computing Series**. *Engineering Agentic AI Systems* brings together a career spent researching, building, reviewing, and teaching consequential systems. Its guiding proposition is that an agent's ability to produce a plausible decision does not grant permission to act: **trusted execution, recoverable state, independent verification, and human accountability must be engineered around it.**
