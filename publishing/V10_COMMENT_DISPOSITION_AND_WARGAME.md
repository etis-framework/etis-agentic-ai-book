## Six reader lenses — acceptance tests
- **New engineer:** terms and refund story build from outcome → delegated work → verified effect; no unexplained E-codes.
- **Architect:** logical responsibility is not a deployment unit; alternatives and authority are separate; avoid mistaking Figure 3.4 for an executable flow.
- **AI engineer:** proposals, authorization, external effects and UNKNOWN are distinct; YAML is illustrative, not enforcement.
- **Security/SRE:** stable operation identity, least privilege, denial/escalation, and reconciliation remain explicit; figures do not grant the model payment authority.
- **Instructor:** Chapter 3 teaches one decision method and a worked refund case; keep detailed runtime mechanisms for Chapters 4, 8–16 and 22–31.
- **Executive/ETIS reviewer:** business ownership, outcomes, accountable approval, evidence and economics are not displaced by model-centric exposition.

## Continuity boundaries
Chapter 1 frames the problem and running case; Chapter 2 establishes inherited engineering patterns; Chapter 3 translates business objectives to bounded delegation. Chapter 4 defines agent mechanisms; Chapter 5 owns economics; Chapter 6 owns uncertainty and authority; Chapter 7 owns requirements; Parts II–VI deepen architecture, implementation, recovery and governance. Reuse refund facts; do not repeat full explanations.
