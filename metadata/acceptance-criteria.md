# Editorial and engineering acceptance criteria

1. The book tells a cumulative story, beginning with business context and the evolution of enterprise engineering.
2. An early-career engineer can follow the concepts and implement the running example; experts can audit contracts and tradeoffs.
3. Each chapter explicitly connects inherited engineering disciplines to modern agentic concerns.
4. Terms and abbreviations are defined; avoid referring to the entire system as “the agent.”
5. CAB-001 is the technical authority; BP-001 is the publishing authority.
6. All 22 inherited figures must pass standalone comprehension, technical accuracy, and final-size readability QA; the explanatory text cannot rescue ambiguous boxes.
7. A combined label such as E2 + E7 is acceptable only when its meaning is explicit inside the figure.
8. Historical and technical claims require source-checked references before publication.
9. Exercises must assess explanation, analysis, design, verification, and defense.
10. Every chapter requires substantive examples, implementation or architectural mechanisms, failures, evidence, and reviewable alternatives.
