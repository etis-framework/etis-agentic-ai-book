# About the Author

**William T. O’Connell, Ph.D.**, is a computer scientist, enterprise software architect, university educator, and former IBM Distinguished Engineer. His perspective on trustworthy intelligent systems reflects four decades of software research, large-scale engineering, technical leadership, and the practical responsibility of evaluating systems that organizations must depend upon.

During more than 25 years at IBM, he held two Chief Technology Officer positions: first in Data Warehousing, Business Intelligence, Analytics, and Big Data within IBM Software; later in Quality and Engineering Delivery Excellence within IBM Consulting, where his technical leadership responsibilities extended across an approximately $15 billion business organization. His work combined enterprise architecture, engineering governance, delivery quality, technical risk assessment, and review of complex client systems. Earlier, he worked on database and distributed-systems development and research at AT&T Bell Laboratories and NCR/Teradata, after beginning his career at Argonne National Laboratory.

He earned his Ph.D. in Computer Science at the Illinois Institute of Technology. He teaches computer science at Loyola University Chicago, including software engineering and advanced data structures and algorithms, and previously taught at Princeton University and the University of Toronto. His work spans both undergraduate and graduate education, connecting foundational computing principles with real architectural decisions, verification, and professional accountability.

Dr. O’Connell is a named inventor on **11 U.S. patents** and has published and presented research and professional work in database systems, distributed computing, and enterprise technology. He founded the **Engineering Trustworthy Intelligent Systems (ETIS)** framework, its reference publications, and the ETIS Professional Computing Series to advance engineering methods that remain durable as AI capabilities evolve.

This book draws on that combination of systems research, enterprise technology leadership, engineering-quality oversight, and teaching. Its underlying conviction is that **an intelligent agent's ability to act is not evidence that it should be trusted to act**: authority, independent enforcement, recoverability, verification, and accountable human governance must be engineered into the surrounding system.
